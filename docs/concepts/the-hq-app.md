# The HQ App

*One small private app a founding team runs the company from. It holds the handful of things founders pass to each other every day, tells each of them the moment the other does something, and is open to every founder's agent as well as to their thumbs.*

---

## What it is

Two founders pass each other the same few kinds of thing all day. Something to raise on the next call. A photo of the whiteboard. A receipt. A link, with the reason it matters. An alert that the product did something. In most companies these travel through a group chat, where they scroll away, and through five tools that each hold one kind.

An HQ app is a single app on each founder's home screen that holds those kinds and nothing else. It is small on purpose. One two-founder company runs on six tiles:

- **Alerts** from the systems the company already watches.
- **To bring up**: a shared list of what each wants to raise with the other.
- **Snaps**: a photo, video or voice note, shared in a tap, with replies by emoji, text or voice.
- **Links**: a link can only be shared with a why, typed or spoken, and the why leads the notification.
- **Expenses**: photograph the receipt, say what it was for, done.
- **A timeline** of the company's days, filled in by each founder's own machine.

A tile the pair does not use is turned off and disappears. The app is private to the people in it. It is the headquarters of a company that may not have an office.

## Every act is one piece of news

The rule that makes it a headquarters is that anything one founder does that the other can see becomes one row of news and one push notification with that founder's name on it. A burst of five snaps is one push. Nothing needs to be announced in chat, because doing it was the announcement.

That changes what founders say to each other. "Did you see the receipt I sent" stops being a sentence. The call starts from the bring-up list, which both of them have been adding to all week, at the moment each thought occurred.

Search covers all of it by meaning. Describing a whiteboard photo from three weeks ago finds it, along with the expense from the same dinner.

## The agents have a key

Each founder's [Chief of Agents](https://supersuit.wiki/concepts/chief-of-agents) holds its own key to the app and acts as that founder. It reads the bring-up list before a call and briefs them. It adds an item when the founder says "remind me to raise that." It files the week's receipts into the company ledger and marks them filed. It shares a link with a why in the founder's own words.

This is the difference from a group chat with good habits. The app is [machine-legible](/concepts/the-machine-legible-business) and [machine-drivable](/concepts/the-machine-drivable-business) from the first day: every tile has a typed record behind it and a door an agent can use. The founders get an app. Their agents get the company's working memory in a form they can read and write. The verbs the agents use to reach it ship as part of [the business plugin](/concepts/the-business-plugin).

## It is the company's own, and it keeps improving

The app is a copy of a shared template, and what makes a copy this company's (its name, its colors, which tiles are on, its own words) lives in one folder the template never overwrites. Improvements to the template reach every copy overnight, and only when that copy's own tests and build pass.

Founders shape it from inside. A menu on every screen lets either of them say what should change, by typing, voice, photo or video, and the suggestion arrives with the screen and context attached. That pattern has its own page: [the in-app suggestion](https://userexperience.wiki/concepts/the-in-app-suggestion). A change one company asks for that every company would want goes back into the template, and the others get it the next morning. This is a [continually improving product](/concepts/continually-improving-products) at the smallest possible scale: two users, both of them the customer.

## When to have one

The test is whether the founders already pass each other these things by hand. If they do, the app costs an afternoon to set up from the template, and it replaces the chat thread where receipts and decisions currently go to be lost.

It is not a project tracker, a wiki or a CRM, and it should not grow into one. The work itself lives where the work lives. The HQ app holds what moves between the people, which is the part of a small company that otherwise lives in two heads and a scroll of messages. Like [the firm super suit](/concepts/the-firm-super-suit), it stays with the company when a founder steps back, with every receipt, decision raised and whiteboard photo still in it.
